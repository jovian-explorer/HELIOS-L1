# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Solar imaging
- HMI maps for the PFSS panel and the occultation field: the JSOC e-mail address entered in the PFSS panel is remembered in the browser and used by both; without it, or with an unknown map source, the answer says that JSOC exports only to an address registered at jsoc.stanford.edu. The occultation PFSS field takes the map chosen in the PFSS panel. (The JSOC search for the Carrington-rotation map was checked; the export needs a registered address and was not run.)
- Event correlation and image archive fixes: HEK flares were cached by the hour, so two events in the same hour shared one list and flares after the event could be listed (now cached to the second and filtered to the 24 h before the event); a CDAW row without a numeric position angle made the whole month fail with an error (now that row is left out); the Helioviewer metadata cache is written atomically and an unreadable copy is fetched again; the 180° correction no longer sends a model with missing values (ray through the Sun, no field model), which was refused.
- PFSS map figure drawn with a matplotlib Figure instead of pyplot, whose global state is not safe when a PFSS request and a chart export run at the same time in the worker threads.
- Occultation robustness: SPICE calls now share the SPICE engine's lock (they had a lock of their own, and the time conversion none, while CSPICE is not thread-safe and both run in the worker pool); an interrupted spacecraft-kernel write no longer blocks that interval for good (the partial file is removed and the kernel always closed); a reversed or too long interval, too many time steps (at most 5000; the run used to stop there without a word) or an HMI map for the field are refused with 422 before anything is downloaded (a bad date came back as 503 and a reversed interval as a Horizons 404).
- Occultation Faraday rotation compared with a post-2006 measurement. A post-2006 case with the PFSS option: Kooi et al. (2014, ApJ 784, 68, doi:10.1088/0004-637X/784/1/68) measured the rotation of 3C228 seen past the north pole on 17 August 2011 at 5 GHz: −21.2, +9.8 and +6.1 rad/m² at 15:42, 19:35 and 23:19 UT (northern hotspot), with the ray's closest approach 5.00, 4.76 and 4.58 R☉ (5.04, 4.79 and 4.61 here). With the PFSS source-surface field of the GONG map of that day (any wind speed from 300 to 600 km/s) the model gives −2.6 to −5.9 rad/m², and with a uniform Parker field ∓2.4 to ∓1.9 rad/m²: the line of sight lies in the unipolar polar field, where the radial field contributes with opposite signs on either side of the closest point and largely cancels, and neither the observed size nor the change of sign is reproduced. Kooi et al.'s own three coronal models give −2.6 to −130 rad/m² and none of them the change of sign either. Numerical checks: the integrals converge (2001 and 40001 samples along the ray agree to 10⁻⁸), the constant is e³/(8π²ε₀mₑ²c³) = 2.63×10⁻¹³ in SI units, and the sign is that of B along the propagation (spacecraft or source to the observer). The models are therefore unchanged: they give the order of magnitude of n B, not the rotation of a given line of sight.
- Predicted L1 arrivals on the L1 time series: the arrival predicted by the corona-to-L1 panel, and by the CME solver when it is started from a measured time, is drawn as a dashed line on the dashboard and archive charts (field, plasma, magnetosphere) wherever it falls inside the time range shown, and kept in the browser until cleared ("Clear arrival markers").
- Corona to L1 (Coronagraph & CME tab, `helios/core/corona_link.py`, `POST /api/link/l1`): a CME from a measured height, time and speed (e.g. the J-map fit, with the drag-based model of the CME solver, Vršnak et al. 2013) or a solar wind stream leaving the source surface at a constant speed (the inverse of the PFSS ballistic mapping) is followed to L1; the predicted arrival and speed are compared with the nearest fast forward shock of the IPShocks database at the chosen spacecraft (WIND, ACE or DSCOVR; any of them if none) within 24 h, and drawn over the speed measured there (NASA CDAWeb, 5-minute means, two days either side). For the CME of 10 September 2017 (C3 front 22.9 R☉ at 17:30 UT, 2480 km/s) the default drag (Γ = 10⁻⁷ km⁻¹, w = 420 km/s) predicts 13 September 07:27 UT at 464 km/s, 12.1 h after the shock observed by WIND on 12 September 19:21 UT (downstream 547 km/s); with Γ = 0.2×10⁻⁷ km⁻¹ it predicts 19 h early at 791 km/s. The CME left from the west limb, so Earth met its flank. The IPShocks snapshot reaches March 2024.
- Event correlation (Coronagraph & CME tab, `helios/core/event_catalogs.py`, `POST /api/events/match`): for a disturbance at a time, height (R☉) and position angle (from solar north through east, seen from Earth), e.g. the closest point of an occultation ray (its position angle is given with the other results, checked against the celestial position angle minus the solar P angle to 0.1°) or a coronagraph front, the CMEs of the SOHO/LASCO CDAW catalogue (Yashiro et al. 2004), CACTus (Robbrecht & Berghmans 2004) and DONKI (NASA CCMC) that reach that height within a window, at their catalogue speed from the catalogue's reference height (CDAW: first height of its height-time file; CACTus: onset taken at 2.2 R☉; DONKI: time at 21.5 R☉), marked by whether the position angle lies in their angular extent (halos, and DONKI cones containing the Sun-Earth line, cover all), and the GOES flares of the NOAA SWPC list (through HEK) and DONKI flares of the day before. For the CME of 10 September 2017 at 15.8 R☉, PA 262 at 16:42 UT (CDAW leading edge 15.77 R☉ at 16:42), CDAW predicts 16:47 and DONKI 16:53, with the X8.2 flare of AR 12673 (peak 16:06) in both flare lists; the CACTus detection starts later (16:48). Catalogues are cached, months older than 40 days for good.
- Radio occultation (Coronagraph & CME tab, `helios/core/occultation.py`, `POST /api/occultation`, `/api/occultation/unwrap`, `GET /api/occultation/options`): SPICE ray geometry for a spacecraft and a ground station (DSN DSS-14 ... DSS-65): spacecraft position at emission (light time), impact parameter of the ray and the heliographic position of its closest point, solar elongation, range rate, geometric Doppler, and a flag when the ray passes through the Sun; along the ray (sampled in the angle from the closest point) the electron column for the Leblanc, Dulk & Bougeret (1998) or Baumbach-Allen (Allen 1947) density, the plasma Doppler shift +40.3/(c f) dTEC/dt and, with a Parker spiral or the PFSS source-surface field, the rotation measure 2.63×10⁻¹³ ∫ n B·dl and the Faraday rotation; the 180° ambiguity of measured polarization angles (jumps removed, then the multiple of 180° nearest the model). The NAIF generic kernels (DE440s, earthstns_itrf93, Earth orientation PCKs; the newest of each name in the NAIF listing) are downloaded once; spacecraft kernels are an SPK from the NAIF or ESA SPICE servers or one written here (SPK type 13) from JPL Horizons vectors. Checked: Mars Express from DSS-63 in the solar conjunction of November 2023, elongation within 0.001° of JPL Horizons (0.1353° at the minimum on 18 November); the electron column of a straight ray agrees with the analytic integrals of the density terms; a purely radial field gives no net Faraday rotation and reversing the polarity reverses it. Compared with published Faraday rotation: Ingleby, Spangler & Whiting (2007, ApJ 668, 520, doi:10.1086/521140) measured with the VLA at 1.5-1.7 GHz the rotation measures of 18 extragalactic sources seen 5.6-9.7 R☉ from the Sun in March-April 2005 (-25 to +61 rad/m², rms 16.5 rad/m²). For the same lines of sight (their source positions and dates) the elongations computed here agree with theirs to 0.1°. At 6.2 R☉ the Leblanc density (1.3×10⁴ cm⁻³) is 0.54 times their LASCO-based value (2.4×10⁴ cm⁻³) and the Parker field (42 mG) lies inside their range of 30-78 mG. With a flat current sheet on the solar equator the rotation measures come out at an rms of 3.6 rad/m² with the wrong sign for 13 of the 18 sources: the size and sign of the rotation are set by where the line of sight crosses the warped current sheet, so the Parker-spiral model gives only the order of magnitude of n B; a model of the actual sheet, such as the PFSS option (GONG maps from 2006 on), is needed for a given date (it could not be tested on these 2005 data).
- Coronal magnetic field, PFSS (Parker Spiral tab, `helios/core/pfss_model.py`, `POST /api/pfss`): the latest GONG (default), ADAPT or HMI synoptic map before a time, read with SunPy and solved with sunkit-magex (source surface 2.5 R☉ by default); the solar source of the wind now at Earth by ballistic mapping (Neugebauer et al. 1998: source-surface longitude = sub-Earth Carrington longitude + Ω(1 AU − r_ss)/V) and the field line traced down from there; field lines from any point between 1 R☉ and the source surface (footpoint, open or closed, polarity, photospheric B_r and a class: coronal hole, edge of or inside an active region at |B_r| ≥ 50 G, quiet Sun); the heliospheric current sheet (B_r = 0 on the source surface) and the angular distance of the connection point from it; a map of B_r with the current sheet and the traced lines. On 9 May 2024 12 UT (GONG) the wind at Earth maps at 450 km/s to an outward-polarity coronal hole footpoint at 39° Carrington, −15°, 47° from the current sheet; the ADAPT and GONG maps of that time agree in longitude (best correlation at zero shift, r = 0.66). A dipole map gives the current sheet on the equator, open polar and closed equatorial field (tests). SunPy and sunkit-magex are optional (`pip install "helios-l1[solar]"`).
- Coronagraph tools (`helios/core/cme_tracking.py`, `GET /api/coronagraph/difference`, `POST /api/coronagraph/jmap|fit`): running-difference images of the archive frames; height-time maps (J-maps, Sheeley et al. 1999) of the difference brightness along a position angle; CME front points by hand on the map or from an automatic track (outermost height above 3σ of the robust noise, outward only, at most 4000 km/s between frames); linear or quadratic height-time fits with errors from the fit covariance; correction of the plane-of-sky speed to radial motion along the source direction, v = v_pos / sin ψ (Vršnak et al. 2007), with the observer at Earth for SOHO/SDO and at the orbit-model position of STEREO-A or Solar Orbiter, not applied within 20° of the line of sight; and the fitted front handed to the CME arrival solver as its start (height, time and speed), which now takes the time of the second height (empty: now). Checked on the CME of 10 September 2017, LASCO C3 at PA 262 (the CDAW measurement angle): automatic front 7.5, 10.3, 12.8, 14.9, 17.1 and 23.3 R☉ from 16:18 to 17:30 UT, 2480 ± 90 km/s, against the CDAW leading-edge heights 9.1 to 25.2 R☉ (2970 km/s fitted the same way): the automatic front lies behind the leading edge measured by eye. On a slower CME (2 October 2021 14:00 UT, C3 at PA 232, CDAW 964 km/s) it lies 0.7 R☉ below the CDAW heights and gives 1043 km/s against 1011 km/s from the CDAW C3 heights. Lower thresholds and smoothing along the line were tried as leading-edge criteria: they bring the September 2017 speed to 2800-2830 km/s but put the October 2021 one at 1300-1480 km/s (picking up streamers), so the 3σ criterion stays and points set by hand remain the way to leading-edge speeds.
- Solar image archive (Coronagraph & CME tab, `helios/core/solar_images.py`, `GET /api/solar_images/sources|frames|image|fits`): images of SDO/AIA (94-1600 Å) and HMI, SOHO/LASCO C2, C3 and EIT, STEREO-A/SECCHI EUVI, COR1 and COR2 and Solar Orbiter/EUI FSI for any interval, from the Helioviewer Project API, played as a sequence. For n frames the images closest to n evenly spaced times are taken, each once and only if inside the interval; every picture is centred on the Sun at a fixed field of view per instrument (±6.5 R☉ for C2, ±30 R☉ for C3, ±15 R☉ for COR2, ±1.3 R☉ for AIA), so the scale (arcsec per pixel) and the solar radius in pixels are known for every frame (checked: the EUV limb of AIA, EUVI and EUI lies 1-4 % outside the computed photospheric radius, as the EUV corona does; the LASCO C2 occulter at 2.4 R☉). Pictures and metadata are cached, for good when more than a day old. The science FITS files of the same instrument and interval are listed and downloaded through SunPy's Fido (VSO, JSOC), an optional dependency (`pip install "helios-l1[solar]"`); without it the panel says so.

### Security
- Cross-site requests to the local server. The server allowed every origin with credentials (CORS `*`), so any web page open in the user's browser could read every API answer and, after the preflight it approved, send JSON POSTs to `127.0.0.1:8765` that changed and saved the settings or replaced the imported dataset; `<img>` or link requests from any site could start month-long archive downloads; and a site whose name resolves to 127.0.0.1 (DNS rebinding) was treated as the server itself. The CORS middleware is gone (the interface is served by the server itself) and a request guard now rejects, before routing: requests whose Host is not 127.0.0.1, localhost or [::1] (403), API requests the browser marks as cross-site or same-site from another port (`Sec-Fetch-Site`) or that carry another `Origin` (403), and POST/PUT/PATCH/DELETE without `Content-Type: application/json` (415), so the form and text/plain bodies a page can send without a preflight never reach an endpoint. Scripts and `curl` (no browser headers) work as before; the interface was checked in Chromium (settings, import, CSV download), and a page on another origin can no longer change the settings.
- Download file names. The export of an imported dataset named its file after the mission name typed in the import tab, unchanged: path separators (`..\..\AppData\...`) and quotes reached the browser's save dialog, a CR/LF in the name injected a response header, and a name outside Latin-1 (e.g. Japanese) made the export fail with a 500. Every download name (exports, reports, analysis JSON, archive and epoch CSV) is now reduced to ASCII letters, digits, `.`, `_` and `-` and quoted. No request value is used as a path on the server: the cache file names come from dataset IDs and hashes, and static files stay inside the static folder (checked with encoded `..` and backslash paths).
- Request limits. Request bodies had no size limit (a 70 MB import was read, decoded and parsed whole), the superposed epoch analysis accepted 60 onsets with 240-hour windows, i.e. 600 days of OMNI files in one request, and the ephemeris endpoint computed every entry of an arbitrarily long, repeated spacecraft list. Imports are now limited to 64 MB and every other POST body to 64 kB (413, counted also for chunked bodies), the mission name to 200 characters, the epoch analysis to 180 days of data in total (events × window, e.g. 60 events of 72 h), and ephemeris requests to 40 distinct spacecraft (repeats are dropped). The import tab says so before reading a file larger than about 60 MB.
- Error answers. A sweep of every GET endpoint with malformed values found 500s: `nan`, `inf` or `1e309` in any numeric query parameter (orbits, ephemeris, Parker spiral, GIC, D-RAP) passed validation and failed JSON encoding, an empty `epoch=` failed in the ephemeris engine, and NaN/Infinity in a JSON body (Python's parser accepts them) made FastAPI's own 422 handler fail. Non-finite numbers now answer 422 naming the parameter, the 422 handler reports the offending input as text, and an empty epoch means now. The CME solver answered 200 with an `error` field for Δt = 0 or r2 ≤ r1, accepted negative drag and any wind speed, and replaced an unreadable launch time by the current time without a word; it now answers 422 for all of these (heights 1-300 R☉, Δt up to 7 days, wind 100-1500 km/s, drag above 0). Saving the settings when the file cannot be written (read-only profile, locked file) still applies them for the session, but the answer now says `"saved": false` with the reason and the settings dialog shows it; it reported `"saved": true` whenever an older settings file existed.
- Bind address and port. `--host` accepted any address, so `helios --host 0.0.0.0` served the API (settings, imports) to the local network; the launcher now refuses anything but 127.0.0.1, localhost or ::1 with a clear message (exit code 2). When the chosen port was taken by another program between the free-port check and the start, the server thread died silently, the launcher saw something answering on the port, printed "Engine online" and opened the window on the other program; it now waits for its own server to report that it is listening and otherwise stops with "could not listen on 127.0.0.1:<port>: the port is in use by another program ... start HELIOS-L1 with --port <a free port>" and the system's reason (exit code 1). The launcher works with `--host ::1` too.
- Stored script injection in the desktop window. The name of an imported dataset (the file name, or the name typed in the import tab) went unescaped into the HTML of the mission directory, so a file named `<img src=x onerror=...>.csv` (a valid name on macOS and Linux), or an import posted by another web site under the old CORS policy, ran script inside the application, where the request guard cannot help. The mission cards now escape every text field, as do the reference search text and the server error messages shown in the timing panel, the fleet matrix, the bulletin and the reference library. Checked in Chromium: the payload ran before the change and shows as text after it.

### Statistics and uncertainties
- Fleet correlation matrix: a pair with no common data, or with a constant series, showed r = 0.000 in the "low correlation" colour, as if the two instruments had been measured to disagree. It now shows n/a.
- Storm finder, solar wind driver: with equal numbers of hours of two types in the six hours before the Dst minimum (3-3 or 2-2-2, common), the storm was always counted as ejecta, the lowest type code, which also biased the driver filter. A tie now goes to the tied type nearest the minimum.
- Dst model skill over two storms: the Student-t factor for one degree of freedom came from a series expansion valid from two up (11.3 instead of 12.71), so the 95 % intervals of the two-storm means were 11 % too narrow. It is now exact.
- OMNI statistics, conditional averages: hours whose value equalled the upper limit fell outside every bin (the last bin did not include its upper edge, unlike the histograms). For discrete quantities such as Kp, whose 99.5th percentile is itself a value, every hour at that value was left out of the last bin. The last bin now includes its upper edge, and logarithmic bins end exactly at their limits.
- Mission comparison lag: the samples left after removing data gaps were packed together, so a shift of k samples spanned the gaps instead of k × 5 minutes, and the correlation at each lag was a sum over all samples divided by their total (smaller for larger lags and for gappy data: 0.76 for two identical, shifted series with frequent gaps). Each lag is now the Pearson correlation of the pairs actually present on the regular 5-minute grid, and the bootstrap interval of the lag uses the mean lagged product over the pairs present.
- Ring current model skill with 95 % intervals. The scores of the Burton and O'Brien-McPherron models against Kyoto Dst (live view) and SYM-H (events, `/api/storm/indices`) now carry intervals for r, RMSE and bias that allow for the autocorrelation of the series: an effective sample size from the lag-1 autocorrelations (Bretherton et al. 1999, eq. 31, for r with the Fisher z interval; Wilks 2011, eq. 5.12, with Student's t for the bias and the mean squared error, the latter on a log scale). Treating every 5-minute sample as independent gave intervals several times too narrow: for 10-12 May 2024 the 864 samples are worth about 3 independent ones, so the answer says that one storm is too few to bound r and gives wide RMSE and bias intervals (O'Brien-McPherron RMSE 64.9 nT, 38-111). The Fisher intervals match scipy's `pearsonr` to 1e-6; simulated AR(1) series (lag-1 autocorrelation 0, 0.5, 0.9) give coverages of 94-96 %, 93-94 % and 90-94 %, and the answer flags fewer than 10 independent samples, where the AR(1) estimate makes intervals too narrow. New module `helios/core/statistics.py`.
- Cross-correlation lag between two spacecraft with a 95 % interval and standard deviation: a moving-block bootstrap (Künsch 1989) resamples blocks of consecutive samples of the lagged products a[n]·b[n+L], re-evaluates every lag on the same resample and finds the peak again (500 resamples, blocks as long as the decorrelation length of the pair and at least the largest lag). In simulations of noisy AR(1) signals with a known 15-minute delay the interval covered the delay in 99-100 % of 200 trials per case, with a bootstrap spread 1.3-1.6 times the actual spread of the estimate, so it is conservative. The comparator's Pearson r now has a 95 % interval as well (Fisher z with the effective sample size of the two series). Both are shown under the values in the comparison tab.
- OMNI statistics (`/api/statistics/omni`, new module `helios/core/omni_statistics.py`): hourly OMNI (OMNI2_H0_MRG1HR, 1963 to the present, one HAPI request per year, cached) with distributions, joint histograms, conditional averages, correlations (Pearson with Fisher interval, and Spearman) and yearly or monthly statistics with the period's sunspot number and the correlation of the period means with it. Intervals use effective sample sizes from the lag-1 autocorrelation of consecutive selected hours (a gappy AR(1) series with 20 % of hours missing: 95 % interval coverage 90-99 % in 300 trials). Filters: conditions on any quantity, southward/northward IMF runs of at least N hours, solar wind type, fine-scale field and plasma points per hour (quality and coverage) and Dst thresholds; the answer, its provenance and the CSV header list every filter with the hours it kept. Derived per hour: Newell dΦ/dt, V·Bs, clock angle, Alfvén speed, specific entropy, Texp/T and the Xu & Borovsky (2015) type from their three planes (as in the FDL 2025 Heliolab code; within 0.5 % of the rounded published inequalities). Checks: daily means of the decoded hourly data equal NASA's published daily averages (omni_01_av.dat) for all 10 955 days of 1995-2024 within the file's rounding, and 27-day means match omni_27_av.dat within rounding for 95 % of 406 rotations (the rest differ by at most 0.1 units, because NASA averages its rounded daily values); 88.5 % of the OMNI hours inside the 106 magnetic clouds of the WIND MFI list (Lepping et al.) are classified as ejecta, against 87.5 % for the same scheme on the magnetic cloud reference set in Li et al. (2020, Earth Space Sci., arXiv:1811.02323), and 91 % of fast wind (V > 600 km/s) outside clouds as coronal-hole plasma. For 1995-2024 the mean speed is 425.8 km/s (95 % interval 419.7-431.9; about 1000 independent samples in 262 000 hours); yearly |B| ranges from 3.9 nT (2009) to 7.6 nT (2002, 2025), r = 0.79 (0.43-0.94) with the sunspot number.
- Ring current model skill over many storms (`/api/statistics/dst_models`, button next to *Find storms*): Burton (1975) and O'Brien-McPherron (2000) run over every hour of the selected years from hourly OMNI (never reset to the observations), scored in each storm window (from the highest Dst in the 24 h before the minimum to the recovery above -50 nT, after Ji et al. 2012), with the means of r, RMSE, bias and minimum error over the storms and Student-t 95 % intervals. Checked against Ji et al. (2012, JGR 117, A03209, Tables 2-3): with their 63 storm windows of 1998-2006, our O'Brien-McPherron gives per-group r of 0.90/0.84/0.87/0.89 (sMC/SH/CIR/non-MC; theirs 0.90/0.80/0.87/0.90) and RMSE 28.2/28.1/19.9/23.8 nT (theirs 26.5/27.6/19.0/20.2), and with our own windows over 84 storms r 0.88 (0.85-0.92) and RMSE 26.4 nT (21.9-31.0). Burton matches in r (0.89/0.78/0.81/0.89 against 0.89/0.71/0.77/0.88) but not in RMSE (57/41/27/41 against 51/56/48/39 nT) or the error of the minimum (-8 nT on average against about +60 nT): their Burton storms are weaker than ours, which use the injection and decay constants of Burton et al. (1975); their constants are not given, so this difference is left as it is and stated here.
- Storm selection by strength (`/api/statistics/storms`) feeding the superposed epoch analysis: runs of hourly Dst at or below a threshold, joined when Dst recovers above it for less than a given time (6 h by default, so that two-step main phases count once), with an optional floor (e.g. intense but not super storms), the plasma type before the minimum (Xu & Borovsky class most frequent in the preceding 6 h), and the mean Bz, speed and Newell coupling of those hours. `/api/analysis/superposed` accepts the same selection instead of onset times, at the Dst minimum or the first hour below the threshold, and writes the selection and its filters (with the storms each kept) into the provenance and the CSV header; the tab's *Find storms* form fills the onset list and keeps the selection while the list is unchanged. Checked against Echer et al.: 1996-2006 at -100 nT gives 90 storms (Echer et al. 2008, JGR 113, A05221: 90); at -250 nT the storms have minima from -263 to -422 nT as in Echer et al. (2008, GRL 35, L06S03), who count eleven where we find ten, the eleventh (15 May 2005, -263 nT in the data of the time) being -247 nT in today's final Dst. In 2015-2024 ten storms reach -150 nT. The plasma type is labelled as a plasma property: sheath and stream-interaction compressions often fall in the ejecta class (81 of the 90 storms of cycle 23), so it is not a driver catalogue.
- Storm finder and Dst model skill: a year of hourly OMNI that could not be read was left out without a word. On 8 October 2026 one request for 1996 failed, and 1996-2006 at -100 nT gave 89 storms instead of 90 (the missing one: 23 October 1996, -105 nT); the answer's provenance named the year, but only the OMNI statistics panel showed it. Both status lines now say "CDAWeb could not be reached for 1996 and nothing is cached: left out" (or which years came from the cache) and turn red; the statistics panel uses the same wording and no longer says "cached data used" for a year with no cached data. Checked with Playwright by editing the answer in the browser to the failed one; the unedited run (90 storms, 75 in a listed ICME, its sheath or the 3 h after it) shows no note.
- OMNI statistics panel in the Event Analysis tab: years, analysis, x and y, bins and log axes, and the filters (conditions, Bz runs, fine-scale points per hour, solar wind types) as form fields; a histogram, a heat map of the joint histogram, the conditional mean with its 95 % band and the quartiles, or the yearly/monthly means with their intervals next to the sunspot number; the correlation and every filter with the hours it kept below the chart, with CSV and JSON downloads. Five example buttons (Dst against the Newell coupling, |B| over the solar cycles, speed distribution, speed against temperature, Bz during intense storms).
- Flux rope fit uncertainties: 1σ errors of B0, R0 (and the diameter), the asymmetry factor, the closest approach, the axis latitude and the axis direction (error cone) from the error study of Lepping, Berdichevsky & Ferguson (2003, JGR 108(A10), 1356), which fitted 1824 simulated clouds noised up with fluctuations from real WIND clouds. The new module `helios/core/lepping_errors.py` ports the WIND MFI team's `interpolation_mc.pro` and its input table `sigcommon.pro` (wind.nasa.gov/mfi): noise level (155/8.3666)·χR, quadratic fits in the noise level, the program's final interpolation in closest approach and cone angle (kept as written), σB0 scaled by B0/16.4 nT. With the WIND table's χR, CA and axis it reproduces Table 5 of Lepping et al. (2006, Ann. Geophys. 24, 215) for 48 of its 50 clouds to the printed precision (the other two differ by 1° in one column at an integer boundary). Shown as ± in the flux rope panel, with a warning when χR, the cone angle or the closest approach lie outside the error study. Rejected before this: errors from the fit itself (linearised χ², residual block bootstrap, boundary shifts of ±1 h), which came out 2-5 times smaller than the published ones, as Lepping et al. (2003) found for random-noise estimates.
- Proton beta and Alfvén Mach number with uncertainties in the coupling analysis (`/api/analysis/coupling`, the same panel): beta_p = 2 mu0 n k T / B^2 and M_A = V sqrt(mu0 m_p n) / B on every plasma sample, with interval means, the 95 % sampling interval and the measurement error propagated from the WIND SWE 1-sigma speed, density and now thermal speed uncertainties (sigma_T / T = 2 sigma_W / W; `Proton_sigmaW_nonlin` is requested with the plasma). WIND 17 March 2015 12-18 UT: beta 0.143 ± 0.0025 (measurement), 95 % sampling interval 0.084-0.201; M_A 4.27 ± 0.015, 3.80-4.74; the propagated errors agree with Monte Carlo draws within 5 %. Against OMNI HRO, after converting to its conventions (beta with alphas and electrons, (4.16e-5 T + 5.34) N / B^2; V_A = 20 B / sqrt(N), which OMNI's own values follow exactly), in the minutes OMNI took from WIND: median ratios 1.10 (beta) and 1.03 (M_A) for 11-12 September 2017, 1.04 and 1.02 for 1-2 September 2018. ### Data acquisition
- 5-minute averages (7-day views): the text fields of the first record (mission, data source, source spacecraft) were copied onto every bin, so a change of the operational L1 spacecraft during the week, or the RTSW tail after archive bins, carried the wrong spacecraft, and the mission comparison could look up the wrong ephemeris. Each bin now takes the labels of its own last record.
- NOAA RTSW feed: when a request failed, the last cached copy was used however old and labelled as current observed data, and the records were not cut to the requested window, so the dashboard, G/R/S levels and the bulletins' "24 h" statistics could describe days-old or longer stretches. The records are now the requested span up to the newest one, and when that is more than half an hour old the provenance says so (with its age).
- Archive day files carry a processing version in their cache key. Day files are kept for good, and the key held only the dataset and parameters, so a change in how a day is processed (quality flags, unit conversions, trimming) never reached days already cached: files written before day files were cut to their own day still held the duplicated minute that made the field and plasma merge fail. - D-RAP panel: the flare absorption card was titled "Polar Aviation ATC Impact" and announced "loss of primary trans-polar ATC communications" above a 15 MHz HAF. X-ray flare absorption acts on the sunlit side; polar routes are affected by solar proton (polar cap) absorption, which the panel does not model. The card now describes the sunlit-side HF loss up to the computed HAF.
- GIC panel benchmarks: Malmö 2003 was given as 3.5 V/km; Wik et al. (2009, Ann. Geophys. 27, 1775) report about 2 V/km at 20:04 UT on 30 October 2003, and the outage description now follows that paper (a relay tripping a 130 kV line, about 50,000 customers for 20-50 min). The 4.8 V/km for Hydro-Québec 1989 and ">10 V/km" for Carrington had no source and are removed; the Carrington card now gives the 4-5 V/km of July 1982 in Sweden (same paper) and NERC TPL-007's 8 V/km benchmark for scale. The hazard bands were attributed to NERC TPL-007 but are HELIOS-L1's own; the GIC response now lists its assumptions (uniform half-space, 180 s period, 1.2 Ω assumed line and winding resistance, own hazard bands).
- Numbers shown before or without data: the page carried example values in its markup (magnetopause 10.4 R_E, ε 14.2 GW, bow shock 13.8 R_E, Parker pitch 45°, event extremes -48.5 nT / 985 km/s / 48 nPa, D-RAP 21.5 MHz / 6.8 dB / R3, "8 logic cores") that stayed on screen as if measured when a request failed; they now show "---" until the server answers. Missing NOAA scales showed "G0 QUIET", "S0 NOMINAL" and "R0 NONE" and a missing Bz "Bz Nominal"; they now say n/a. Gaps in the latest record were printed as "null" in the dashboard gauges. The speed status called 480-500 km/s "elevated" while the fast-wind filter and the exported chart use 500 km/s; it now uses 500. The sandbox's "sync from live" replaced missing live values with 450 km/s, 5 cm⁻³, 6 nT, Bz -3 nT and 10⁵ K without saying so; it now keeps the sandbox value for a missing field and names it.
- Auroral map, for an oval at a past time: the day/night terminator, subsolar point and station MLT were drawn for the current time, and the overlay showed "KP 3.5" and "HPI 45 GW" fallback numbers when no Kp was used. The map now follows the oval time, and the overlay shows the |AL| that drives the oval and "n/a" for Kp and power when they were not used (found in the end-to-end run).
- Error messages: invalid parameters (FastAPI validation errors, which come as a list) reached the user as "[object Object]" or raw JSON; the interface now shows them as "field: message". An unexpected server failure answered a plain-text "Internal Server Error" page, so the interface failed on a JSON parse error; it now answers JSON naming the endpoint and the exception, and the traceback is logged.
- Settings are kept between runs. The desktop window ran in pywebview's default private mode, so theme, font size, time zone, core count and the incident log were forgotten at every start, and the server reset the core count to 4 while the slider showed the old value. The window now keeps its browser storage in the config folder, the server saves the settings to `settings.json` and restores them at start-up, and the interface takes the saved values from the server. `POST /api/settings` rejects unknown themes, font sizes and time zones and out-of-range core counts with a message (HTTP 422) instead of storing them or, for the core count, ignoring the value silently.
- Coronagraph tab: the "Active Region Flare Scanner" showed a fixed list of four 2024 regions (AR 3664, 3697, 3842, 3848) at invented disk positions, with invented nicknames and flare probabilities (e.g. "C 99% M 85% X 45%"), on an animated drawing labelled "SDO / AIA 193 Å" as if it were the current Sun. It now plots the numbered regions of the latest NOAA SWPC solar region summary (location, Mount Wilson and McIntosh class, area, spot count, flares of the day and SWPC's 24 h C/M/X and proton probabilities; new `/api/solar/regions`). When the summary cannot be loaded, the 2024 regions are shown as labelled historical examples at schematic positions, without probabilities. The disk is labelled as a schematic drawing and the colour buttons as drawing styles ("193 Å style"); the 171 Å style gave Fe IX as 1 MK (it forms near 0.6 MK). When a live LASCO or SUVI image cannot be loaded, the bundled placeholder drawing is now marked as such instead of appearing under the instrument name.
- Faster cold start of the fleet heatmap (and every view that downloads several products at once): each download built its own TLS context, loading the CA bundle for about 0.35 s on the event loop, and large NOAA products were parsed and re-encoded to the cache on the event loop as well. One TLS context is now shared, and the response bytes are parsed and cached in a worker thread. Measured with cProfile and wall-clock timing on an empty cache: the fleet matrix took 8.5 s (16-19 s under the profiler, 5.7 s of it in `load_verify_locations` and 7 s in `json.dump`) and now takes 2.7-3.5 s, most of it network time; a warm request stays at 0.55 s.
- Dashboard regime filters: "Fast wind (> 500 km/s)" and "Sheath (N > 10 cm⁻³)" filtered at 480 km/s and 8 cm⁻³, and with a filter the MIN/AVG/MAX rows described a different set of samples than the large latest values without saying so (e.g. latest 275 km/s above a filtered minimum of 286). Thresholds now match the labels and a note states how many samples the filter keeps, or that none match and all are shown.
- Spectrum tab: the "log-log" PSD plot had a linear (category) frequency axis, so the f^-5/3 and f^-3/2 references were curves and the spectrum crowded at low frequency; the frequency axis is logarithmic and tooltips give the frequency.
- Comparator scatter plot: the regression and 1:1 lines turned the x axis into a category axis, so every pair was drawn at the left edge in one vertical stripe; the axis is numeric again.
- Hazards tab text: the GIC formula was shown as raw LaTeX source, and the flare absorption panel credited Sauer & Wilkinson (2008), whose paper covers D-RAP's solar-proton (polar cap) absorption; the panel now states the SWPC flare relation it uses and that the proton part is not modelled.
- Hazards tab: the 10 MHz attenuation card always said "~79.1% signal power loss" whatever the flare; it now shows 1 - 10^(-A/10) for the computed attenuation (e.g. 76.6% for 6.3 dB).
- Forecast tab: the Dst card and axis were labelled "Dst*" and "Burton model"; the card shows the current (observed) Dst and the trajectory comes from the O'Brien-McPherron model, and the labels now say that.
- Orbit view: the flaring-parameter card still cited the old Chao et al. (2002) DOI and implied both α came from that paper; it now names Shue et al. (1998) for α_mp and the Chao bow shock (or the Farris & Russell conic) for α_bs, with the correct DOI.
- Auroral oval: the map label computed its own equatorward midnight boundary (68 - 1.8 Kp, e.g. 63.2°) while the card showed the server's value (60.9°); the map now shows the server's value. The boundaries come from a linear Kp/Bz approximation, not the Starkov (1994) model they were labelled as; the labels say so.
- Solar imagery: the EUV panel and the feeds labelled "SDO / AIA 193/304" were GOES SUVI images, and their URLs had moved (the browser blocked the 404 page). They are labelled GOES / SUVI 195 and 304 Å with the current SWPC URLs. The "Aditya-L1 / VELC" feed showed the SOHO LASCO C2 image; VELC has no public real-time image (ISSDC PRADAN requires a registered login), so the entry now says so instead of showing another instrument's picture.
- Opening the Data Import tab before anything was imported logged a console error: `/api/custom/latest` answered 404. It now answers `{"status": "empty"}`.
- Reference library: every DOI was checked against Crossref. Several pointed to unrelated papers and are corrected: Burton et al. (1975) now cites the JGR paper the Dst model comes from (10.1029/JA080i031p04204; the Science paper stays on the E_y parameter), Boyle et al. (1997) 10.1029/96JA01742, Sauer & Wilkinson (2008) 10.1029/2008SW000399 with its actual title, and the mission papers for DSCOVR (Burt & Smith 2012, IEEE Aerospace), Aditya-L1 (Current Science 2017), WIND (Acuña et al. 1995), SOHO (Domingo et al. 1995), GOES-16 (Kress et al. 2020, SEISS), SDO (Pesnell et al. 2012) and Solar Orbiter (Müller et al. 2020). - The "processors" setting is now used: it sizes the worker-thread pool that runs archive decoding, power spectra, cross-calibration and the fleet matrix off the server's event loop (it was stored but never used). The settings text describes what it actually controls.
