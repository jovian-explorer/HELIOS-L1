# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Event analysis
- Interplanetary shock analysis on archive data at the instruments' own cadence (WIND MFI 3-s with SWE non-linear proton fits, ACE MAG 16-s with SWEPAM 64-s Level 2, DSCOVR MAG 1-s with the Faraday cup), not the 1-minute display means: shock normals from magnetic and velocity coplanarity and the three mixed-mode methods (MC, VC, MX1-3; Schwartz 1998, Abraham-Shrauner 1972), the shock speed from mass flux conservation, theta_Bn, density and field jumps, the upstream normal flow speed and Alfvén and fast Mach numbers, with uncertainties from Monte Carlo draws of the window means. The shock time can be refined to the largest |B| jump, the type (FF/FR/SF/SR) follows from the jumps, and the result carries warnings when the jump or the sample count is too small. Checked against the Harvard-Smithsonian CfA Interplanetary Shock Database: for the 17 March 2015 shock at WIND the MX3 normal is within 1° of the published one, theta_Bn 60.5° (CfA 60.1 ± 3.1°), shock speed 540 km/s (536 ± 3 km/s), compression 2.48 (2.45 ± 0.46), arrival within 3 s of the published time; for 27 February 2014 the normal is within 1° of the CfA MX3 normal, theta_Bn 78.7° (79.9 ± 3.8°), 439 km/s (436 km/s), arrival within 3 s; for 22 June 2015 the normal is 2.5° from the CfA's selected Rankine-Hugoniot normal, theta_Bn 63.3° (63.8 ± 5.0°) and the speed 762 km/s (median of the CfA methods 791 km/s). `GET /api/analysis/shock?mission=&time=&upstream_minutes=&downstream_minutes=&exclude_minutes=`.
- Shock analysis details: WIND SWE spectra flagged "poor peak identification" (fit_flag 1) are used and counted in a warning. Behind strong shocks most spectra carry this flag (all downstream spectra of 27 February 2014 and 6 September 2017), and the CfA database, built by the SWE team, uses them: its downstream values are those of the flagged spectra. The refined shock time is where |B| crosses half-way between the levels before and after the jump, not the largest difference of the 1-minute means, which could fall on the overshoot (ACE, 22 June 2015: 17:59:37 instead of 18:00:09). The "poorly constrained normal" warning compares MX3 with the other methods that use the velocity jump; magnetic coplanarity alone degenerates for quasi-perpendicular shocks (the CfA MC normal of 27 February 2014 is 70° from their MX3 too).
- Minimum variance analysis of the magnetic field for any interval of up to 72 h, with the eigenvalues, eigenvectors, eigenvalue ratios and the angular errors of Sonnerup & Scheible (1998, eq. 8.23): `GET /api/analysis/mva?mission=&start=&end=&average_seconds=`.
- Multi-spacecraft timing between WIND, ACE and DSCOVR: the shock normal and speed found at one spacecraft predict the arrival at the others from their measured positions, t = t_ref + n·Δr/V_sh, and the observed arrival (the |B| ramp near the prediction), the residual and the speed along n implied by the observed delay show whether the shock is planar over the baseline. Positions come from the WIND MFI file's definitive orbit (the SWE file gives positions to 1 R_E and the predicted WIND orbit file differed by 2 R_E), ACE MAG Level 2 and the DSCOVR orbit file. On 27 February 2014 ACE, 10 R_E further upstream than WIND but 140 R_E away in Y, saw the shock 17 minutes after WIND: the WIND normal predicts a 1016 s delay (1028 s from the CfA database's own normal, speed and positions), the ACE ramp is observed 1103 s after WIND, within the CfA ACE database's arrival time and its ±80 s, and the ACE position agrees with the CfA's to 0.04 R_E. `GET /api/analysis/timing?reference=WIND&time=&targets=ACE,DSCOVR`.
- Magnetic cloud flux rope fit: the force-free Lundquist model (B = B0 [J0(αr) axial + H J1(αr) azimuthal], αR0 = 2.405) is fitted to the field directions of 30-minute averages for the axis direction, closest approach and its time, for both handedness signs, with B0 from the full vectors and R0 from the duration and measured speed (Lepping et al. 1990). Checked against three quality-1 clouds of the WIND MFI magnetic cloud table with the table's boundaries: 18-20 October 1995 axis 9° from the published one, B0 24.4 nT (25.9), right-handed, closest approach 9 % (12 %), diameter 0.271 AU (0.257), axial flux 13.7×10²⁰ Mx (13.1), χR 0.149 (0.143); 10-11 January 1997 axis 13° off, B0 16.9 nT (17.7); 4-6 March 1998 axis 18° off, B0 12.6 nT (12.4), left-handed as listed. `GET /api/analysis/fluxrope?mission=WIND&start=&end=&average_minutes=30`.
- Superposed epoch analysis of OMNI 1-min data keyed on any list of onset times (up to 60, e.g. sudden commencements or shock arrivals): SYM-H, ASY-H, AE, AL, AU, IMF, speed, density, temperature, dynamic pressure and ε on a common epoch grid with mean, median, quartiles and the number of events per bin, the per-event series, and each event's extremes with their epoch times. Checked on the sudden commencements of 17 March 2015 and 10 May 2024: the per-event SYM-H minima are the published -234 nT at 22:47 UT and -518 nT at 02:14 UT (Tulasi Ram et al. 2024) at epoch +18.03 h and +9.15 h, and SYM-H jumps by more than 40 nT within 5 minutes of epoch zero. `GET /api/analysis/superposed?onsets=2015-03-17T04:45,2024-05-10T17:05&parameters=sym_h,bz_gsm,speed&before_hours=12&after_hours=48&cadence_minutes=10` (`format=csv` for the table with a metadata header).
- Event Analysis tab (Data Studio & Science): forms for the shock analysis and multi-spacecraft timing, minimum variance analysis, the flux rope fit and the superposed epoch analysis, with the methods table, the field and density around the shock with the averaging windows marked, observed and model field of the flux rope, the epoch median, mean and quartile band per quantity, and links to save each result as JSON (and the epoch table as CSV). Buttons load the CfA-checked shocks of 17 March 2015, 27 February 2014 and 22 June 2015; the other fields start at the checked magnetic cloud and storms. Times are entered and shown in UTC. Checked in the browser (Playwright) against the live CDAWeb server: every panel returns the values listed above, server errors such as an unreadable time are shown as text, and the tab fits a 420 px wide window. A timing target without any data for the interval (DSCOVR before 2015) is reported as such instead of "no position".
- The answers include a provenance block (CDAWeb datasets with the exact HAPI requests, sample counts and coverage, method, parameters, software version, retrieval time) and are available as a JSON file with `download=true`.

### Data acquisition
- 7-day DSCOVR and ACE views: the RTSW minutes inside the archive's last 5-minute bin were averaged into that bin together with the archive value, so the last archive point mixed two data sets (e.g. 380 km/s from CDAWeb and four RTSW minutes near 560 km/s gave 526 km/s). RTSW now starts at the first bin after the archive's last one. Found by a new test of the archive/RTSW join.
- Solar cycle panel: the "smoothed sunspot number", F10.7, cycle 25 peak ("~172") and the cycle 24 curve were an analytic sin² rise / exponential decay curve (cycle 24 as 0.68 times it) presented as the current values and labelled as Hathaway's model; the "polar field reversal underway" card was fixed text. The panel now shows NOAA SWPC's observed monthly and 13-month smoothed sunspot number (SILSO) and F10.7, the observed cycle 25 maximum (smoothed SSN 160.9 in October 2024), cycle 24 from the observed record aligned on its December 2008 minimum, and NOAA's predicted cycle with its range. The analytic curve remains as the offline fallback, labelled MODEL; the polar field card, which had no data behind it, now shows the observed maximum.
- Aditya-L1: checked whether ASPEX/SWIS or MAG Level-2 data can be fetched without a personal login. The ISSDC PRADAN landing page is public, but every data page redirects to the ISSDC sign-in, the PRADAN FAQ says data are available after registering, and CDAWeb has no copy. Aditya-L1 therefore stays a labelled simulation; the mission entry points to PRADAN and the provenance text says that a registered login is required.
- Geomagnetic indices: OMNI 1-min SYM-H, ASY-H, AE, AL and AU (WDC Kyoto) are read from OMNI_HRO_1MIN with the solar wind and carried in OMNI records and exports. The live auroral view now plots the observed WDC Kyoto real-time Dst (hourly, from NOAA SWPC) next to the simulated Dst, drives the model with L1 solar wind shifted to Earth by its travel time, starts it from the observed value and reports r, RMSE and bias against the hourly values. Historical events show observed SYM-H against both ring current models driven by OMNI, and `/api/storm/indices?start=&end=` returns the indices and the same model-vs-SYM-H comparison for any interval of up to 31 days. Archive day files are now cached per dataset parameter list, so files cached before a parameter was added are fetched again.
- The NOAA `/products/solar-wind` plasma and magnetometer JSON products used for "live" data now return HTTP 404, so every live view had been showing synthetic model output without saying so. Live solar wind now comes from the NOAA SWPC RTSW 1-minute feeds. The DSCOVR view shows the operational L1 stream (the supplying spacecraft, e.g. SOLAR1, is recorded per record); the ACE view shows real ACE data.
- Every record has a `data_source` label (`observed`, `simulated`, `synthetic`, `imported`) and every API response a `provenance` block (description, source spacecraft, UTC coverage, cadence). The feed badge in the dashboard shows which kind of data is on screen.
- Missing values stay null. Previously missing field values became 0 nT and missing plasma values were replaced by 400 km/s and 4–5 cm⁻³ before computing derived quantities.
- 7-day views are 5-minute block averages instead of every fifth sample.
- HTTP error pages and non-JSON responses fall back to the cached copy; cache files are written atomically.
- NOAA G/R/S scales use observed planetary Kp, GOES X-ray and ≥10 MeV proton flux instead of a Kp guessed from Bz and fixed X-ray (1.5e-6 W/m²) and proton (1.2 pfu) values.

- Real archive data from NASA CDAWeb through its HAPI server for WIND (MFI 1-min, SWE), ACE (MAG and SWEPAM Level 2, then preliminary key parameters), the DSCOVR science archive (1-s magnetometer from 2015, Faraday cup 2016-2019) and OMNI 1-min HRO. Dataset IDs and parameter names were checked against the live HAPI catalog. Declared fill values and samples flagged bad by the instrument teams are dropped; data are reduced to 1-minute means and cached per day (days that ended more than two days ago are kept, recent days are re-checked every six hours).
- New `/api/archive/{mission}?start=&end=&cadence_minutes=` endpoint (JSON or `format=csv`) returns measured data for any interval of up to 31 days, with the datasets used, their level and coverage in the provenance. `/api/archive` lists the sources.
- WIND is no longer simulated when the archive has data for the window. The 7-day DSCOVR and ACE views use archive data where it exists and the RTSW feed only after the archive's last sample, instead of showing just the ~24 h RTSW window. When the archive has nothing yet (it lags real time by days to months), the view says so and keeps the labelled fallback.
- Dashboard: the Observation Horizon menu has an "Archive Interval (NASA CDAWeb)" choice with source, UTC start/end, cadence and CSV download; the feed badge names the CDAWeb datasets (and RTSW) behind observed data, and the event viewer states whether the profile is observed or synthetic and shows the observed extremes.
- Historical events (May 2024, St. Patrick's Day 2015, Halloween 2003, Bastille Day 2000) show 48 h of real OMNI data as 5-minute means, with the observed extremes next to the published values. Events without OMNI coverage (March 1989, Carrington 1859) and the quiet baseline remain illustrative synthetic profiles, labelled as such.

### Scientific corrections
- NOAA G scale: the classifier started G1 at Kp 5.0, so Kp 5- (4.67) was reported as G0, 6- as G1, 8- as G3 and so on. NOAA counts a "minus" value with its integer (G4 is "Kp 8, including a 9-"), and SWPC's own Kp product tags 4.67 as G1. The levels now start at 5-, 6-, 7-, 8- and 9o; the forecast hazard cards use the same levels.
- Physics calculator (sandbox): the bow shock models were fed the proton-only magnetosonic Mach number (V_A = 21.8 B/√N, C_s = 0.117 √T), while the orbit view and the panel text use the OMNI definitions (V_A = 20 B/√N, C_s = 0.12 √(T + 1.28×10⁵)), so the same conditions gave different bow shocks in the two views (the proton value is 10-15% higher); "Send to orbit view" passed the proton value on. The bow shocks and the badge now use the OMNI M_ms (also available as `SpaceWeatherPhysics.omni_magnetosonic_mach`); the wave speed table keeps the proton values, as `/api/physics` does. `/api/physics` computed M_A and M_ms from wave speeds already rounded to 0.1 km/s and differed from the calculator in the second decimal. A new test runs the browser calculator in Node for four regimes and checks every quantity (P_dyn, wave speeds, Mach numbers, β, Shue 1997/1998, Chao and Farris-Russell bow shocks, ε, Newell, Boyle, E_y, AE proxy) against the Python implementation. The AE proxy 0.048 (dΦ/dt)^0.85 + 25 nT was listed under Newell et al. (2007), which does not give it; it is now labelled as a rough HELIOS-L1 scaling, and the duplicate Newell (2007) entry in the reference library is merged.
- Storm forecast tab: the "AI / ML" forecaster had no machine learning in it. Its Kp was an ad hoc blend (0.6 (1.8 + dΦ/dt / 7000) + 0.4 (1 + |Dst|/22)), the "95% CI" band was an invented ±(0.3 + 0.12 h), the G1/G3/G5 storm probabilities were linear ramps of that Kp, the auroral boundary was 68 - 1.9 Kp, the satellite card quoted an invented drag multiplier (1 + 0.45 (Kp - 3)), the aviation card announced polar cap absorption from Kp (it needs a solar proton event) and the Newell coupling was given in Wb/s (its units are (km/s)^4/3 nT^2/3). Kp now comes from the Newell et al. (2008) solar wind fit, Kp = 0.05 + 2.244×10⁻⁴ dΦ/dt + 2.844×10⁻⁶ n^½ v² (coefficients as implemented in JHUAPL's kaipy), the band is the range of that Kp over the last 3 h of data and is labelled so, the boundary is Starkov (1994) for that Kp, and the probabilities are NOAA SWPC's own forecast for the next UTC day (minor G1, moderate G2, strong-extreme G3-G5), with NOAA's predicted 3-hourly Kp drawn on the chart. Without NOAA's forecast the gauges show n/a. The persistence assumption behind the 12-hour trajectory (drivers relaxing to quiet values by 5 % per hour) is listed under the chart. The forecast endpoint answers 503 instead of a forecast built from default values when no record has speed and Bz.
- CME solver presets, checked against the SOHO/LASCO CDAW CME catalogue: "May 2024 (~1800 km/s)" matched none of the CMEs behind the 10 May 2024 storm; it now uses the 9 May 09:24 UT halo CME at its catalogue linear speed of 1280 km/s, the fastest of the 8-9 May CMEs that merged on the way. "Halloween 2003 (~2150 km/s)" is now the 28 October 11:30 UT halo at 2459 km/s, and Bastille Day gives the catalogue 1674 km/s exactly (it gave 1675). Carrington 1859 and Nominal are marked illustrative (there are no coronagraph data for 1859), and each button's tooltip names the catalogue entry. The shock Mach number and the Kp category of the solver stay as labelled rough estimates: no published relation gives either from a plane-of-sky speed alone.
- Hemispheric power: the auroral tab, map overlay and reports gave 10^(0.21 Kp + 0.5) GW, a relation with no source (5.1 GW at Kp 1, 245 GW at Kp 9). It is now the Zhang & Paxton (2008) TIMED/GUVI relation HP = 16.82 exp(0.32 Kp) - 4.86 GW up to Kp 7, with the linear ramp to 300 GW at Kp 9 that TIE-GCM and WACCM use above Kp 7 (checked against Smith-Johnsen et al. 2018, JGR, eq. 1-2, and `hp_from_kp` in the TIE-GCM source): 18.3 GW at Kp 1, 153 GW at Kp 7. `/api/aurora` returns the relation in `hemispheric_power_source`, and the reference library lists the paper. Before the first answer from the server the auroral map overlay showed Kp 3.5, 42 GW and a boundary latitude from an ad hoc formula; it now shows n/a.
- CME solver: the value labelled "Gopalswamy et al. (2001) transit" was the Sun-L1 distance divided by a mean speed of (u + 400)/2 km/s with a 10-hour floor. The published empirical arrival model is now implemented: effective acceleration a = 2.193 - 0.0054 u m/s² until 0.76 AU, constant speed afterwards (Gopalswamy et al. 2001 abstract via Crossref; acceleration from Ravishankar & Michalek 2019, arXiv:1910.02797). It reproduces the ~4.3-day arrival of slow CMEs the paper reports. The panel shows its 1 AU arrival time and speed next to the drag-based model, whose ambient wind and drag parameter can now be set (they were fixed at 420 km/s and 1×10⁻⁷ km⁻¹, and the propagation animation used 1.8×10⁻⁷ km⁻¹ and ignored the server's wind speed). The panel text credited the drag deceleration to Gopalswamy; it now names Vršnak et al. (2013). The benchmark presets gave other speeds than their labels ("May 2024 (~1800 km/s)" computed 2824 km/s, "Bastille Day (~1675 km/s)" 2128 km/s); their Δt now gives the labelled speed. The response lists the solver's assumptions (no projection correction, rough shock Mach number, Kp category from speed only). The reference entry has the paper's real title and the cessation distance.
- Auroral oval: the Starkov (1994) model replaces the linear Kp/Bz approximation. The poleward and equatorward boundaries of the oval and the equatorward edge of the diffuse aurora are computed in corrected geomagnetic latitude from |AL| and magnetic local time with Starkov's coefficients, read from the page image of Sigernes et al. (2011, Table 2) and identical, signs included, to the copy in the open `auroraloval` package; the boundaries agree with the published Feldstein-Starkov oval plots (UNIS thesis, Fig. 2.10) to the 1.5° they can be read. The driver is the OMNI 1-min AL averaged over the hour before the oval time when OMNI covers it, otherwise Kp converted with Starkov's |AL| = 18 - 12.3 Kp + 27.2 Kp² - 2 Kp³ nT, or a value you enter. |AL| is limited to 18-750 nT: above about 780 nT the fitted equatorward boundary crosses the poleward one. Bz no longer moves the boundaries (the model does not use it). Typical values change: at Kp 7 the equatorward midnight boundary is 59.9° CGM (was below 52°). The map draws both boundaries and the diffuse edge from the server for the oval time, placed with the IGRF-14 centred dipole and the magnetic local time of the subsolar point (it drew a Kp ring around a fixed pole); the cursor and station MLT use the same dipole (the old MLT was about 3.5 h off at Tromsø). The aurora tab has oval time and AL inputs, shows the AL used and its source, and `/api/aurora` takes `time` and `al`; for a past time without OMNI AL, Kp or AL it answers 404 instead of drawing today's oval. The hemispheric power card says it is a Kp estimate.
- Ring current (Dst) models. The model labelled "Burton" combined O'Brien & McPherron's (2000) injection with Burton's fixed 7.7 h decay. Both published models are now implemented separately and selectable: O'Brien & McPherron (2000), Q = -4.4(VBs - 0.49) nT/h, τ = 2.4 exp(9.74/(4.69 + VBs)) h, b = 7.26 nT/nPa^½, c = 11 nT (default), and Burton et al. (1975), Q = -1.5e-3 nT s⁻¹ per mV/m above 0.5 mV/m, decay 3.6e-5 s⁻¹ (7.7 h), b = 0.20 nT (eV cm⁻³)^-½ = 15.8 nT/nPa^½, c = 20 nT. Coefficients were checked against Ji et al. (2012, JGR, NASA NTRS 20140006636), the 2025 Earth and Planetary Physics paper on semi-empirical Dst (epp2025072) and the PREDSTORM code. The forecaster's "current Dst" came from an ad hoc formula (6.5 Bz V/400 - 1.5 P_dyn); it is now the latest observed Kyoto Dst, and the 12-hour trajectory steps the O'Brien-McPherron model from it.
- Bow shock and magnetopause models. The formulas labelled "Chao et al. (2002)" were not that model: the magnetopause used (11.12 + 0.057/0.541 Bz) P^(-1/6.6) with a P-linear flaring term, and the bow shock (14.5 + 0.08 Bz) P^(-1/6.6) times an ad hoc Mach factor, with clamps. The bow shock now uses the published Chao et al. (2002) model, r = r0((1 + ε)/(1 + ε cos θ))^α with ε = 1.029 and r0, α depending on P_dyn, Bz, plasma β and the magnetosonic Mach number M_ms (14 coefficients). The functional form was checked against Zhou et al. (2020, Chin. J. Space Sci.) and Mo et al. (2025, JGR, open access); the implementation reproduces the 13.325 R_E standoff Mo et al. list for the original coefficients. Chao et al.'s magnetopause coefficients are not available in any open source, so the magnetopause is now Shue et al. (1998) in every view. The second option is the Farris & Russell (1994) bow shock on the Shue et al. (1998) magnetopause (the relation OMNI uses for its bow shock nose, checked against the OMNI documentation and Tátrallyay et al. 2012) with the Farris et al. (1991) shape; it replaces a Shue-shaped shock with a fixed α = 0.60. The orbit view's Shue flaring still used ln(P + 1); it now shares the corrected formulas. M_ms and β are computed from the measured speed, density, temperature and |B| with the OMNI definitions; the orbit view had used a fixed Mach number of 8 because telemetry records carry no Mach field, and the sandbox fed the Alfvén Mach number to the bow shock. The sandbox compares Shue (1998) with the Shue (1997) standoff OMNI uses. `/api/magnetosphere/boundaries` takes `plasma_beta`, rejects unknown models and names the magnetopause and bow shock models separately. The Chao et al. (2002) DOI in the reference library was wrong (now 10.1016/S0964-2749(02)80212-8).
- Akasofu ε lacked the 4π/μ₀ factor of the SI form and was 12.6 times too small (500 km/s, 10 nT southward: 994 GW, previously 79 GW).
- Shue et al. (1998) flaring exponent used ln(P_dyn + 1) instead of ln(P_dyn) (API and in-browser calculator).
- Flare class is the real GOES class (e.g. X5.3); before, it was the R-scale threshold label ("X1" for an X5.3 flare, "B" for A-class). Without proton data the S-scale says so instead of using an invented 0.8 pfu.
- Spectrum tab: the sample interval is taken from the time stamps (it was fixed at 60 s, so 5-minute data had all frequencies 5× too high); gaps are interpolated instead of spliced out; the PSD is one-sided and window-normalised in (unit)²/Hz.
- Dst (Burton/O'Brien–McPherron) simulation used a fixed 5-minute step, so 1-minute data ran five times too fast. The step now comes from the time stamps and uses the exact solution over each step.
- The comparator's spatial baseline and theoretical delay use the measured position (NOAA RTSW hourly ephemeris) of the spacecraft that actually supplied observed RTSW data, e.g. SOLAR1 for the operational "DSCOVR" stream, instead of the mission's model orbit. On live data for SOLAR1 vs ACE (173,000 km apart along X) the theoretical delay (10.0 min) and the cross-correlation lag (10 min) agree. The position source is reported for each side.
- Cross-correlation lag had the opposite sign to the theoretical propagation delay it is compared with. Positive now means spacecraft B observes later than A.
- Comparator no longer bridges long data gaps with interpolated values that were then counted as real samples; mixed time zone formats can be compared.
- A single interplanetary shock was reported up to three times; it is now one event.
- GSE Z axis in the ephemeris engine pointed to ecliptic south (left-handed frame), flipping the sign of every GSE z. Orbit trajectories returned the same point for every step.
- Boyle et al. (1997) polar cap potential uses the IMF magnitude |B| (it used √(By² + Bz²)).
- Newell coupling in the forecaster used |B| instead of the transverse field B_T = √(By² + Bz²).
- D-RAP absorption follows the SWPC relation HAF = 10 log₁₀(Fx) + 65 MHz (reduced by cos^0.75 χ) and A(f) = (HAF/f)^1.5 dB.

### Import and export
- Exports: the PNG overview chart did not say whether it showed measurements or model output (a simulated Aditya-L1 chart looked like data); it now prints the data kind and source under the title, its fast-wind line sits at the 500 km/s the dashboard uses (it was an unlabelled 450 km/s), and it no longer switches matplotlib's global style for the whole process. The CSV export of imported data gets the same `#` metadata header as the other CSV exports (title, data kind, import warnings, coverage, units).
- Import tab presets: the five storm and quiet-wind tables are hand-written illustrative profiles, but were listed with instrument names ("DSCOVR / NASA OMNI-HRO", "ACE SWEPAM / MAG", "ACE SWEPAM / SOHO CELIAS"). They are now labelled as illustrative profiles (API `data_kind: "synthetic"` with a note), the imported dataset is named "(illustrative profile)", and the panel points to the Archive Interval loader for measured OMNI data.
- File import reads the formats it claimed: JSON (record lists, NOAA SWPC products with a header row, HELIOS-L1 JSON exports) had always failed with "Dataset must contain at least 2 columns", and HELIOS-L1's own CSV exports could not be imported again because of their `#` metadata lines (now treated as comments). Column headers with units or spaces such as `Bz GSM (nT)` or `Speed [km/s]` are recognised; before, they were ignored without a word and every value came out empty. A file without any recognised column is rejected with the list of accepted names, unused columns are reported, and the import warnings (dropped rows, fill values, GSE to GSM rotation, unused columns) are now shown in the import tab; they were returned by the server but never displayed. The file picker offers text and JSON files and explains that CDF/NetCDF files have to be listed as text first instead of failing on binary content.
- Files with only GSE field components are no longer left without Bz-based quantities: By and Bz are rotated to GSM for the time of each row (Hapgood 1992, with the dipole axis from the IGRF-14 degree-1 coefficients), and Bx is shared by both systems. The rotation reproduces the GSM components published in OMNI to their 0.01 nT rounding. GSE columns are kept in the imported records; files with their own GSM columns are used as they are.
- File import: OMNI/CDF fill values are treated as missing, missing columns are no longer filled with nominal values, time stamps are converted to UTC and sorted, OMNI YEAR/DOY/HR/MN columns are read, and the import is vectorised.
- CSV export includes a metadata header (provenance, coverage, cadence, units) and handles records with differing fields; NetCDF-style schema units are correct.
- Reports state the actual provenance of the data they summarise and no longer fail on a trailing data gap.

### Interface and robustness
- Storms archive: clicking a second event while the first was still loading (an OMNI download takes seconds) could leave the first event's data and cards under the second event's highlight. A slower answer for an earlier selection is now dropped (found in the end-to-end run).
- Historical event cards: the Bz, speed and pressure cards showed fixed "published" values without a source while the measured OMNI extremes were only mentioned in the text, and several were wrong: Bastille Day 2000 gave 1675 km/s as the peak solar wind speed (that is the coronagraph CME speed; OMNI peaks near 1140 km/s at L1), Halloween 2003 gave Bz -52 nT and 55 nPa (OMNI 5-min: -28.5 nT, 12.5 nPa), May 2024 -48.5 nT (OMNI -44.3 nT). For events covered by OMNI the cards now show the OMNI 5-minute extremes of the 48 h window; the offline fallback profiles are built from those values, and for March 1989, Carrington and the quiet baseline the cards say they show the illustrative profile inputs. The St. Patrick's Day 2015 peak Kp is 8- (7.67), not 8, and the severity badge uses the NOAA levels.
- Ephemeris epochs: an epoch the engine could not read (e.g. a typo in `?epoch=`) silently became a fixed date in September 2026 and the orbit and SPICE views answered for that date. `/api/orbits`, `/api/ephemeris/spice` and `/api/ephemeris/spice/trajectory` now answer 422 with the expected format. New tests check the engine's analytic fallbacks against SPICE itself: the Kepler solver reproduces `spiceypy.conics` to better than 1 m for three orbits including a Parker Solar Probe-like perihelion passage, the leap-second-free ET agrees with `str2et` to 2 ms since 2017, and the fallback GM values match the bundled kernel; the PNG export is checked as a valid image that leaves matplotlib's global style alone.
- Constants shared by the browser and the server now agree: the Parker spiral view used a solar rotation rate of 2.87×10⁻⁶ rad/s in the browser and 2.865×10⁻⁶ rad/s on the server, so the pitch angle and footpoint shown in the same card changed by 0.1° depending on which wrote last; the CME animation placed L1 1.5 million km from Earth (the solver 1.496 million km) and started with a drag parameter of 1.8×10⁻⁷ km⁻¹ (server default 1×10⁻⁷). A test checks these constants against the Python models.
- D-RAP panel: the flare absorption card was titled "Polar Aviation ATC Impact" and announced "loss of primary trans-polar ATC communications" above a 15 MHz HAF. X-ray flare absorption acts on the sunlit side; polar routes are affected by solar proton (polar cap) absorption, which the panel does not model. The card now describes the sunlit-side HF loss up to the computed HAF.
- GIC panel benchmarks: Malmö 2003 was given as 3.5 V/km; Wik et al. (2009, Ann. Geophys. 27, 1775) report about 2 V/km at 20:04 UT on 30 October 2003, and the outage description now follows that paper (a relay tripping a 130 kV line, about 50,000 customers for 20-50 min). The 4.8 V/km for Hydro-Québec 1989 and ">10 V/km" for Carrington had no source and are removed; the Carrington card now gives the 4-5 V/km of July 1982 in Sweden (same paper) and NERC TPL-007's 8 V/km benchmark for scale. The hazard bands were attributed to NERC TPL-007 but are HELIOS-L1's own; the GIC response now lists its assumptions (uniform half-space, 180 s period, 1.2 Ω assumed line and winding resistance, own hazard bands).
- Numbers shown before or without data: the page carried example values in its markup (magnetopause 10.4 R_E, ε 14.2 GW, bow shock 13.8 R_E, Parker pitch 45°, event extremes -48.5 nT / 985 km/s / 48 nPa, D-RAP 21.5 MHz / 6.8 dB / R3, "8 logic cores") that stayed on screen as if measured when a request failed; they now show "---" until the server answers. Missing NOAA scales showed "G0 QUIET", "S0 NOMINAL" and "R0 NONE" and a missing Bz "Bz Nominal"; they now say n/a. Gaps in the latest record were printed as "null" in the dashboard gauges. The sandbox's "sync from live" replaced missing live values with 450 km/s, 5 cm⁻³, 6 nT, Bz -3 nT and 10⁵ K without saying so; it now keeps the sandbox value for a missing field and names it.
- Auroral map, for an oval at a past time: the day/night terminator, subsolar point and station MLT were drawn for the current time, and the overlay showed "KP 3.5" and "HPI 45 GW" fallback numbers when no Kp was used. - Error messages: invalid parameters (FastAPI validation errors, which come as a list) reached the user as "[object Object]" or raw JSON; the interface now shows them as "field: message". An unexpected server failure answered a plain-text "Internal Server Error" page, so the interface failed on a JSON parse error; it now answers JSON naming the endpoint and the exception, and the traceback is logged.
- Settings are kept between runs. The desktop window ran in pywebview's default private mode, so theme, font size, time zone, core count and the incident log were forgotten at every start, and the server reset the core count to 4 while the slider showed the old value. The window now keeps its browser storage in the config folder, the server saves the settings to `settings.json` and restores them at start-up, and the interface takes the saved values from the server. `POST /api/settings` rejects unknown themes, font sizes and time zones and out-of-range core counts with a message (HTTP 422) instead of storing them or, for the core count, ignoring the value silently.
- Coronagraph tab: the "Active Region Flare Scanner" showed a fixed list of four 2024 regions (AR 3664, 3697, 3842, 3848) at invented disk positions, with invented nicknames and flare probabilities (e.g. "C 99% M 85% X 45%"), on an animated drawing labelled "SDO / AIA 193 Å" as if it were the current Sun. When the summary cannot be loaded, the 2024 regions are shown as labelled historical examples at schematic positions, without probabilities. The disk is labelled as a schematic drawing and the colour buttons as drawing styles ("193 Å style"); the 171 Å style gave Fe IX as 1 MK (it forms near 0.6 MK). When a live LASCO or SUVI image cannot be loaded, the bundled placeholder drawing is now marked as such instead of appearing under the instrument name.
- Faster cold start of the fleet heatmap (and every view that downloads several products at once): each download built its own TLS context, loading the CA bundle for about 0.35 s on the event loop, and large NOAA products were parsed and re-encoded to the cache on the event loop as well. One TLS context is now shared, and the response bytes are parsed and cached in a worker thread. Measured with cProfile and wall-clock timing on an empty cache: the fleet matrix took 8.5 s (16-19 s under the profiler, 5.7 s of it in `load_verify_locations` and 7 s in `json.dump`) and now takes 2.7-3.5 s, most of it network time; a warm request stays at 0.55 s.
